Multimodal Cultural Artifact Analysis

The player message system in Elden Ring

This project examines how players communicate through short preset messages and how meaning changes depending on location, context, audience, purpose, player interpretation, in-game situation, and community interaction.

Project Purpose

Analyze how limited language, location, ratings, and shared experience work together to create meaning in the Elden Ring player community.

Primary Research

Two semi-structured interviews provide perspectives on context, trust, creativity, and community knowledge.

Artifact #1 – Writing as Knowledge-Making & Primary Research

This artifact is a reading response about “Writing Is a Knowledge-Making Activity” by Estrem and Driscoll’s discussion of primary research. In this activity, I connected the idea of writing as a way of creating knowledge to my Multimodal Cultural Artifact Analysis about the player message system in Elden Ring. I learned that writing can help generate new questions instead of only recording ideas I already have. I also began thinking about how interviews could help me understand how other players interpret messages differently. This artifact connects to the Generating Inquiry learning outcome because it helped me move from describing my own experience to developing questions that could be explored through research.

Original Activity

According to Estrem, writing does not only record ideas that we already have. It can also help us create new ideas and understand things better while we are writing. Estrem explains that “writers use writing to generate knowledge that they didn’t have before.” This means that writing can be part of the thinking process instead of only being the final result.

Interviews could help me understand the player message system in Elden Ring from other players’ perspectives. I already have my own experience with the messages, but another player might understand certain jokes, warnings, or phrases differently. Driscoll explains that interviews are useful when you want detailed information from a small number of people, so interviewing other Elden Ring players could give me more specific examples and opinions about how the message system works in the community. One question I might ask is: “How do you decide if an Elden Ring message is meant to help, joke, or trick another player?” One challenge I expect is that different players may interpret the same message in different ways, so it could be difficult to organize their answers or decide which ideas are the most important.

Entry 01ePortfolio Check-In

ePortfolio Check-In #1

Student Learning Outcomes: Generating Inquiry and Multiple Ways of Writing

Generating Inquiry (GI)

One activity that helped me develop Generating Inquiry was the reading activity about “Writing Is a Knowledge-Making Activity” and “Introduction to Primary Research: Observations, Surveys, and Interviews.” Before this activity, I mostly thought about my Elden Ring artifact from my own experience as a player. However, the readings made me realize that writing and research can help me discover ideas that I did not already have. The activity also made me think about how interviews could give me different perspectives from other players. For example, I became interested in asking how players decide whether a message is meant to help, joke, warn, or trick someone. This helped me move from simply describing the message system to asking questions about how different players interpret it and how meaning is created in the community.

Multiple Ways of Writing (MWoW)

The activity about “Writing Is a Social and Rhetorical Activity” helped me understand Multiple Ways of Writing because it made me think more about audience, purpose, and context. In Elden Ring, players communicate through short preset messages, but the meaning of those messages also depends on where they are placed and what is happening in the game. A message near a dangerous area can work as a warning, while the same type of message somewhere else could be a joke. For MA1, I can use screenshots and examples of messages along with my written analysis to show how text, visuals, location, and game context work together. This activity helped me understand that communication is not only about the words themselves, but also about how they are presented and interpreted by an audience.

Entry 02ePortfolio Activity

ePortfolio Activity – Check-In #2

Student Learning Outcomes: Information Literacy and Research Genre Production

Information Literacy (IL)

The activities in this unit helped me become more careful about how I find and use information. When researching my core value, I looked for scholarly sources that were directly related to writing studies instead of choosing sources only because they mentioned a similar topic. I learned to consider the author, relevance, credibility, and how a source could contribute to my understanding of the core value. My primary research also helped me see the topic from another perspective. Interviewing Participant One showed me that people can interpret and respond to the same type of writing differently depending on their experiences and context. I also became more aware of ethical choices, such as paraphrasing accurately, citing sources, and protecting the identity of participants. Chapter 3 connects to this because it explains that an ePortfolio should consider audience, privacy, accessibility, and the ethical presentation of other people's information.

Research Genre Production (RGP)

This unit also helped me understand that research writing requires more than collecting and summarizing sources. Through the activities related to scholarly research and annotated bibliographies, I learned that an annotation should explain not only what a source says, but also why the source is useful and how it connects to my research. I had to make choices about which information was most relevant and how to organize it clearly for an academic audience. This helped me move from simple summary toward analysis and connection between sources. Chapter 3 also helped me think about my ePortfolio as another genre with its own audience and purpose. The organization, language, accessibility, and artifacts I choose all affect how readers understand my development as a writer and researcher.
